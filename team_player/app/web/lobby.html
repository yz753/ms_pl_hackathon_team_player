Beyond the usual position.
Beyond goals and assists.

A striker drops deep. A fullback joins the attack. A winger runs without the ball into an unusual position to draw defenders away. What does the team gain, and what does it give up?

Team Player turns player and ball positions into online analysis and personalised Azure AI explanations for audiences with all levels of football knowledge. It focuses on strikers, fullbacks and wingers, including the benefits and costs of moving away from their usual positions.

The match viewer shows players and the ball moving on the pitch, statistics updated as each frame arrives, and AI narratives generated during playback. You can follow a player, explore a similar situation from a previous season where a player made a different choice, and compare two players head to head. AI reports take about 10 seconds to generate. They are not prerecorded.

Simulated matches

All matches are synthetic. No real match data or player tracking is used. Matches follow the Premier League schedule structure: 20 teams, A–T, play across 38 matchweeks. Each matchweek contains 10 fixtures, with every team playing once. Each team meets every other team twice, once at home and once away, giving 380 fixtures in a complete season.

Each simulated game is a 10-minute segment, starting with goalkeeper possession or possession elsewhere on the pitch. This simplifies the demonstration. It is not a full 90-minute fixture. The analyser processes frames incrementally, so ten minutes is not a limit imposed by live analysis speed. Playback defaults to 2× and can be changed to 1×.

Historical season: 2021–2022

One past synthetic season, comprising 380 matches, was generated and analysed offline. These historical data are stored in Azure Blob Storage and used for comparison with current-season games streamed and analysed online.

The comparison looks for players in the same position, from a different team, facing similar ball possession and team shapes. It compares their observed decisions and what followed, helping explain how different choices may affect the game. Similar situations are not identical, so these comparisons are for reference only and do not conclusively prove cause and effect.
